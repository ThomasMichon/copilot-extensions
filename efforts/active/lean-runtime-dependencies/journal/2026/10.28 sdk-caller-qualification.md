## Native offer fixture attribution

- The new current-source qualification remains negative: both 16-case SDK
  packets passed, then the first native offer control failed its process-wide
  handle-count equality assertion. Seven remaining offer and four startup cases
  were unrun, not skipped. The single calibration, exact raw foreground
  restoration, continuous quiet capture, native publications and original-owner
  settlement are retained as component observations, not overall acceptance.
- A separately authorized non-visible diagnosis compared two cold contexts,
  each with one child: plain standard-library exit and the frozen SDK's
  no-permission path. Both produced the same raw counter sequence:
  `190, 201, 200, 199, 193, 193`. Releasing all six observed process/stream/reader
  objects coincided with a six-handle drop; one explicit GC collected zero
  objects and changed no count. The residual three handles remain unidentified.
  The SDK child demonstrably refused at its actual permission gate. This does
  not identify all resources or prove the complete production-versus-fixture
  cause, but the aggregate failure is not specific to Job offering.
- The fixture correction scopes the raw counter to the SDK operation: allocate
  file-backed capture and process/I/O resources first, keep their handles and
  references held, and independently release a cold SDK child only after taking
  the baseline. Require exactly one correlated, non-inheritable offered Job
  where expected, then exact return to that same baseline after its known
  closure, before disposing the fixture. The original decision bytes, explicit
  parent identity, mode/case identities and unknown-custody rules remain intact.
  No unknown offset, tolerance, warmup or GC workaround is introduced.
- That fixture-only change left production API/runtime sources and the frozen
  negative evidence unchanged.
  Portable dependency-double controls and a Linux cold-gate control cover the
  fixture mechanics; they do not establish native Windows acceptance. A fresh
