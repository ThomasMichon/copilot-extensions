## Failure boundary

A release-requested ordinary Bridge attempt can reach physical body absence
while its original task remains queued and its conversation is not retired.
The supervisor retires that attempt, then processes its retained housekeeping
envelope. A pending session-end component can remove the managed Bridge handle
and its turn rows, even though its keyed journal and native/workspace history
still need them. Failed attempt, absent controller, retired conversation and
terminal task are different facts.

## Existing owner correction

The queue now reads current task need inside its existing cleanup transaction.
For an unretired ordinary local Bridge conversation whose task is non-terminal
or unavailable, it grants no cleanup lease. That refusal returns the unchanged
reservation without a token, retry consumption, history rewrite or repeated
error. The existing end checkpoint, cleanup-lease revalidation and outcome
publication independently recheck that custody.

Exact physical absence still permits the attempt's normal retirement and
capacity release. Its conversation and pending housekeeping remain retained
until the original lifecycle permits cleanup. Terminal tasks or explicitly
retired conversations still face all existing host, allocation, wake, startup
and cleanup guards. Other drivers retain their previous admission. No new
daemon, timer, command, storage schema, executable pin or recreation path is
introduced.

This closes an existing lifecycle/preservation intent rather than extending
the vision. It neither restores already-missing Bridge metadata nor proves a
new activation, second turn or closeout of an original native conversation.
Those recovery and runtime acceptance gates remain open.
