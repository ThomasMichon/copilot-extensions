## Requested outcome

Use the remaining backlog as a graph, identify coherent dense themes and link
issue groups to efforts that retain their work. Continue finding duplicates
and already-resolved outcomes, including semantic comparison against completed
pull requests and commits. Small documentary publication is a delivery
mechanism, not the main research engine or a proxy for backlog reduction.

The existing graph and admitted research are inputs, not evidence that the
current semantic graph, receiving fit or consolidation has already happened.
The numerical checkpoint and every original author, requirement, ownership
and safety constraint remain.

## Agent-recommended execution

1. **Build a current guarded corpus.** Keep every open issue as a graph node,
   including metadata-only excluded or held nodes. Qualify original authors,
   current editors, all historical edit authors and complete discussion
   connections before description access. Do not feed held, locked, excluded,
   incomplete or changing text to the embedding model. Preserve point-in-time
   observations; a graph snapshot does not authorize later provider effects.
2. **Measure index coverage first.** A code/commit mirror does not imply
   coverage of the original host's issues or merged PR descriptions.
   Reuse a suitable existing embedding service without shared-index writes,
   reindexing, model installation, service repair or unrelated workload changes.
   Compare all candidate types in the same model space.
3. **Build weighted issue relationships.** Use bounded whole-document chunking
   with separately retained original and discussion evidence. Semantic neighbour
   weights nominate alignment, not duplicate equivalence. Keep explicit
   dependencies, conflicting alternatives, direct inheritance and separate
   custody as typed relationships rather than converting every mention to
   an undirected theme edge.
4. **Rank coherent groups.** Measure internal weighted density, external
   conductance, coverage, isolates and representative outcomes. Check multiple
   neighbour thresholds and community resolutions for brittle groupings.
   Suppress generic provenance hubs and document boilerplate sensitivity.
   Density alone is neither priority nor expected closure yield.
5. **Match completed work separately.** Retrieve candidate merged PR descriptions
   and commit summaries, retaining repository identity, actual merge status,
   source date and scope. Report corpus/time coverage explicitly. Separate
   implementation, planning, release and installed evidence; a documentation
   merge or similar commit title cannot demonstrate technical completion.
6. **Create effort-sized consolidation packets.** Select the strongest existing
   suitable effort before proposing a new one. List the group's actual issues,
   common unfinished outcome, unique residuals, dependencies, current custodian,
   completed credit and recommended first slice. Keep rejected/uncertain members
   and independent OPEN tokens visible. Deliver a coherent group, not another
   inventory of unrelated leaf amendments.
7. **Adjudicate before effects.** Independently verify high-scoring duplicate or
   resolved-work candidates against complete current requirements and real
   evidence. Review and read back complete receiving acceptance before any
   supersession. Existing source-specific conditions and custody survive;
   similarity, density, a theme label or an issue list alone grants no closure.

## Validation and useful outputs

- Account for the entire observed queue: semantically processed, held/excluded,
  changing/incomplete, ungrouped and isolated nodes.
- Produce a reconstructible weighted graph and ranked community table with
  actual issue membership, density/conductance and sensitivity observations.
- Publish the strongest effort-sized groups with actual existing-home fit,
  residual acceptance and no silent changes to implementation ownership.
- Produce an issue-to-merged-work candidate table with scores, source/merge
  provenance, evidence tier and independently accepted or rejected disposition.
- Distinguish algorithm-produced candidates from coordinator-reviewed semantics.
  Keep original descriptions and discussions available for complete adjudication;
  centroid embeddings do not preserve every acceptance clause.
- Measure receiving groups, duplicate/resolved candidates, verified closures
  and net queue change separately. Do not count graph nodes, new documents or
  external departures as campaign closure credit.

No product implementation, native execution, deployment, cleanup, source/
resource transfer or shared-index maintenance is authorized by this method.
