## #5301 - Preserve the process-per-unit architecture and actual child custody

Retain durable per-managed-child exclusion across master generations. The
accepted invariant is at most one lease-owning supervisory loop, not an absolute
process count: brief losing contenders and temporarily zero processes are
allowed. Keep the deliberate separate subprocess per unit and sibling fault
isolation; master singleton and memory-only unit tracking are insufficient.

Retain startup discovery/adoption, safe reconciliation or authorized retirement
before conflicting supervision. Unconfirmed retirement stays visible and blocks
conflicting launch. Preserve a diagnosed real child outcome and durable health/
logging evidence with success, return code, error and duration or equivalents,
and trace actual hook/watchdog/ensure/reconcile launch paths.

The later refinement corrects the historical discarded-stream diagnosis: its
inspected launcher no longer explicitly discarded stdout/stderr. Inherited
streams or optional master progress still do not establish durable child
outcomes. Spontaneous queue recovery is not proof of permanent deadlock or repair.

Preserve the accepted #4236 transfer: drain/retire superseded generations and
reconcile each declarative logical registration to exactly one active runtime
unit, crediting delivered singleton, handoff and equivalent-registration dedupe.
Keep declaration provenance, stable logical identity versus exact immutable
spawning generation, local vetoes, non-expiring task/resource custody, reversible
direct-to-declared migration and conflict diagnostics. Adoption must retether
the child before old-owner checks can retire it.

Retain generation-bound owner tether, identity-plus-owner validation, both
termination guards, shared lifecycle primitives, stated platform behavior,
ambiguous-owner refusal and visible retry/manual seams. Age, version or missed
progress is not termination authority; PID reuse after kernel-lease release
must not block forever. Existing supervised-lane and fleet-recovery owners
retain their composed responsibilities; no competing watchdog is introduced.

The owning effort's simultaneous-contender, owner-death/uncertainty, adoption,
live-orphan restart, refusal and actual-child-outcome regressions remain.
Optional dogfooding is supplemental, not a mandatory every-host campaign.

## #10231 - Native lifetime is independent of single-supervisor ownership

The original author claims the shared Linux durable-launch boundary. When a
resident originates inside a systemd cleanup scope, establish an independent
user-owned native scope before application execution. A new POSIX session
alone does not remove caller-cgroup lifetime. Refuse unsupported escape rather
than silently accepting it.

Preserve attributable instance/PID custody, literal argv/environment, redirected
logs, ordinary non-systemd operation and explicit test containment. Do not weaken
caller cleanup or add an unrelated supervisor. The original regression includes
an isolated native-unit retirement where the same resident survives and a
bounded control does not, plus refusal and contained/non-systemd controls.
Existing live residents retain separately qualified migration duties.

The selected merged message reports precisely relevant independent-user-scope
work and finite native fixtures; do not describe source work as absent.
It is not independent verification of the full original or live migration.
The exact associated merged PR description was wholly withheld before rendering
because protected-reference preflight failed. No source, neighboring subset or
alternative fetch was used to bypass that boundary.
